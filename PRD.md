# Envoggle — Product Requirements Document

**Release** · Emory Hacks MVP  
**Audience** · Friends with iPhones who want a quick competitive game over FaceTime  
**Submission** · Entertainment main track and Chert Best Use of FaceTime side challenge

## Product overview

Envoggle is a live 1v1 scavenger hunt. Challenge a friend in iMessage, meet in FaceTime, and race to find the rarest object that fits a shared category. Both players can see their own rear-camera view and their opponent’s live view while they search. Seeing someone find a better object is what makes it tempting to abandon a safe pick and keep looking.

Chert brings an AI referee into the Group FaceTime call. The referee gives both players the same category, runs the round, scores what each player shows on camera, and reveals the winner. The target experience is two players and one referee in a single call.

## Player experience

1. **Challenge a friend.** Open Envoggle in the iMessage app drawer and send a branded **1v1 Challenge** card. The friend taps the card and selects **Accept Challenge**. Both players get the same round code in the invite. The hackathon build assumes the iMessage extension is installed on both demo phones.
2. **Join FaceTime.** After acceptance, a player starts Group FaceTime and brings in the other player and the Chert referee using the call flow verified on the assigned line. Accepting the card and starting the call are separate user actions. The host reads the round code to the referee so it can load the correct challenge. [Apple Messages framework](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/messages) · [Apple Group FaceTime](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/make-a-group-facetime-call-iph405ab67de/ios)
3. **Hunt for 20 seconds.** Once both players are ready and both rear cameras are on, the referee announces the category and starts the countdown. Both camera views stay visible on both phones. The referee displays the category and timer on its tile and calls out five seconds remaining. The first demo category is **Things that are blue**.
4. **Lock in and confirm your find.** While the timer is running, point your rear camera at an object and say **“Lock it in.”** The referee saves a fresh frame from your camera and acknowledges the lock. You can keep hunting and lock in a replacement before time expires. Both live feeds remain visible. At the buzzer, the referee judges each player’s last saved frame. A player who never locked in receives zero points. If the referee’s best guess matches the dictionary, it asks the player to confirm it aloud. If there’s no dictionary match, or the player rejects the initial guess, the referee offers three distinct guesses based on the frame and a **“None of these”** option. The player can say an option number or supply their own item name aloud. The referee verifies the selected or supplied name against the same frame. If the item remains unclear or unconfirmed, the entry receives zero points. If the referee loses either camera feed during the hunt, void the round and offer a restart.
5. **Reveal together.** The referee’s tile shows both objects, scores, and the winner at the same time. The referee reads a short explanation aloud. A tie is a draw. Either player can request a rematch in the same call, and the next round starts after both are ready.

## Scoring requirements

- **Confirm the object before scoring.** Gemini proposes a label from the locked frame. After the player confirms the label, Gemini checks category eligibility against that frame. For **Things that are blue**, blue must be a prominent part of the object. A small blue logo on a white mug or a photo of a blue object displayed on a screen earns zero. An unclear image also earns zero with a short explanation.
- **Prepare a scoring dictionary for each category.** Start with an AI-generated draft of candidate objects, canonical labels, aliases, and proposed rarity tiers. Before making a category playable, the team manually reviews the draft, adds missing objects, merges synonyms, and adjusts tiers based on how hard each object would be to find in a 20-second hunt around campus. Eligible finds earn **1 point** for common, **3 points** for uncommon, or **5 points** for niche. For **Things that are blue**, a pen is common, binoculars are uncommon, and a violin is niche.
- **Score the confirmed item.** Resolve the verified label against the category dictionary, including aliases. A match earns its preset score. If the eligible item is still unlisted, Gemini assigns **1, 3, or 5 points** using the category’s existing rarity tiers and dictionary examples as anchors. The referee gives a short reason for the score, and Envoggle flags the item for manual review between playtests. Both players use the same dictionary for the round. Higher points win. If scoring fails or exceeds 10 seconds after both entries are resolved, offer a replay and record the round as void. Time spent confirming an item is outside that scoring window.

## Confirmed frames and training data

- Save the locked image and its player-confirmed object label as a pair for each player who opts in to training-data retention. Explain before the hunt that Envoggle may license or sell these labeled images to third parties for vision-model training. Players can decline and still duel.
- Keep frames temporarily for judging and the reveal. Only the final frame with a confirmed label and the player’s opt-in enters the training dataset. Exclude replaced frames and continuous call footage. Strip image metadata and exclude frames with identifiable people or visible personal information from the training dataset.

## Technical requirements and first test

- **Messages** · Build the iMessage extension in Swift. Use `MSMessage` and `MSMessageTemplateLayout` for the challenge card and `MSSession` for a follow-up acceptance card. Store an opaque round code with the invite and keep match state on the backend so reopening the card loads the current status. [Apple interactive messages](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/messages/mssession)
- **FaceTime and Chert** · FaceTime carries player video. A TypeScript LiveKit agent connected through Chert handles the referee’s audio, scoreboard tile, timer, frame lock-in, and scoring. The agent needs current, attributable frames from each player for scoring. Chert’s public docs describe a single `facetime-remote-video` track. Access to two independently attributable feeds in one group call is unverified. [Chert caller vision](https://docs.trychert.com/facetime-cli/video) · [Chert line setup](https://docs.trychert.com/facetime-cli/connect)
- **First test** · On the assigned video-enabled line, put two actual iPhones and the referee in one Group FaceTime call. Confirm on both phones that each player can see their own rear-camera preview and the opponent’s feed throughout the hunt. Show different objects simultaneously, then swap them, and confirm the agent captures and attributes both correctly without relying on who is speaking. Verify the referee’s timer and score are legible in the chosen FaceTime layout. Repeat across two calls on venue Wi-Fi.
- **Demo contingency** · If the group call or video bridge fails, preserve the two-way live duel with two equal camera tiles in a browser arena and a plain iMessage join link. Demo that as the Entertainment build. Treat Chert sponsor eligibility as unverified for the fallback.

## MVP acceptance and schedule

- **Invite** · Both demo phones can open the same challenge, accept it once, and resolve to the same round. A plain invite can substitute for the custom card if extension signing or installation threatens the demo.
- **Duel** · Both phones show both live feeds during a shared 20-second hunt. Lock a frame, then point the camera elsewhere and verify the saved frame is judged. Verify a second lock replaces the first, an attempt after the buzzer is ignored, and no lock earns zero. Both scores reveal together within 10 seconds of resolving the entries. An invalid item, a tie, and a dropped feed produce the specified outcomes.
- **Scoring** · Test at least 12 sample captures across the three rarity tiers, invalid items, blurry images, and eligible unlisted items. Verify that dictionary entries and aliases earn their preset score, clearly invalid entries earn zero, and verified unlisted entries receive an AI-assigned tier with a short reason.
- **Confirmation and retention** · Verify the referee asks both players to confirm their labels before scoring. For an unlisted item, test choosing one of three distinct guesses and supplying a custom name through **“None of these.”** Verify the referee checks both paths against the locked frame and that only the final confirmed image and label from an opted-in player enter the training dataset.
- **Friday** · Confirm group calling, caller-camera access, and the FaceTime layout first. Use the first two build hours as the go/no-go window for the FaceTime path.
- **Saturday** · Deliver one end-to-end round before polishing the invite. Test on the demo phones and record the two-minute submission video by Saturday evening.
